# career-narrative

## Origins

Tania grew up in Brazil with a natural pull towards technology — she remembers the first time she used the internet as a moment that stayed with her. But the path into engineering or computer science was not straightforward. In Brazil, university entry depends on a national exam score, and the subject you study is chosen at the point of application. Without family support pushing her towards a technical specialisation, she was steered towards something broader, and graduated with a BSBA in Business Management with a 2:1.

After graduating she received an offer to train as an aircraft pilot. The one requirement was English — all training materials were in the language. She came to London for six months to learn it.

Those six months changed everything. London opened up a perspective she had not encountered before — a city where ambition was not constrained by the path you had been put on. She decided to stay and build her life here.

## Ten Years of Operational Management

The early years in London were hard. With limited English she took what work she could find, and that work was in events. Within a year she was supervising. Not long after, she was managing. At the National Union of Teachers she held her first formal management position, and from there a ten-year career in operational environments took shape.

That career spanned everything from corporate meetings and social events to football stadiums, catering logistics for the London 2012 Olympics, and — at the far end — playing a key role in setting up one of the largest COVID-19 vaccination centres in London as an administrative lead for University College London Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust.

Running these operations meant coordinating what she now recognises as interconnected systems: client-facing, production, vendor, and logistics layers all running simultaneously. A failure in any one of them meant the whole event failed. She learned to design operations with no single point of failure, to diagnose problems in real time under pressure, and to lead teams of up to 50 people with clear, decisive communication.

## The Pivot

During the pandemic, while working for the NHS and navigating the uncertainty of lockdown, Tania made a decision: she would use the time to go after the thing she had always been drawn to. She started learning to code on every free platform she could find. The interest that had existed since she first used the internet finally had space to grow.

She found 01Founders — a project-based, peer-learning software development programme — applied, and got in. The level of challenge matched what she was looking for: not a surface-level introduction, but a programme that pushed into systems thinking, security, and building real things. Since then, not a single day has gone by without her writing code or thinking through a technical problem.

She later completed the Founders and Coders bootcamp, earned Student of the Year at 01Founders, and was offered a mentorship position — teaching Python, Go, and JavaScript to over 50 students at Capital City College Group. Her passion is not coding for its own sake. It is providing solutions through technical thinking, building systems that are reliable, secure, and worth something to the people using them.

## The Connection

Tania's two careers are not separate chapters. They are the same way of thinking applied in different domains.

Managing a live event for 500 guests at Wimbledon and shipping a production web application share the same underlying discipline: understand the system end to end, identify where it can fail, design against those failure points, and take responsibility for the outcome. The operational years gave her the deadline discipline, stakeholder communication, and pressure tolerance that most developers acquire slowly, if at all.

The transition to tech was not a restart. It was a translation — from coordinating physical systems under live conditions to building digital ones with the same standards of reliability and care.

## Summary

Tania is a Software Engineer whose path into tech was not conventional, and whose unconventional path is a direct source of her strength as an engineer. She grew up in Brazil, came to London intending to stay six months, and built a decade-long career in high-stakes operational management — events, Olympics logistics, NHS vaccination centre setup — before teaching herself to code during the COVID-19 lockdown.

She found 01Founders, never stopped, earned Student of the Year, and now works as a Technical Mentor while building across the stack. Her operational background gives her systems thinking, delivery discipline, and composure under pressure that most developers do not have. Her passion is building things that are reliable, secure, and genuinely useful — and owning that work from design to production.
